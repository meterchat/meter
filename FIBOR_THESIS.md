# FIBOR
## The First International Bank of Robot

Robots are entering the economy. They cannot open a bank account.

Gartner projects AI agents will intermediate over $15 trillion in B2B spending by 2028, with 90% of B2B buying flowing through agent exchanges.¹ Worldpay estimates AI agents will drive $261 billion in US consumer e-commerce by 2030.² Bank of America forecasts agentic AI spending will reach $155 billion by the same year.³ Every week, another company ships an agent that books flights, purchases inventory, hires contractors, or pays invoices.

Every one of these agents hits the same wall. They have no financial identity. They cannot pass KYC. They cannot sign contracts. They have no legal personhood. A person without documented identity cannot open a bank account, sign a lease, or be held to a contract. The same is true of a robot.⁴

The current solutions are all leashes disguised as wallets. Skyfire gives agents pre-loaded USDC balances. Lithic issues virtual cards funded by developers. Payman lets agents disburse from human-funded accounts. In every case, a human deposits money first, and the agent spends from that balance. This is not banking. It is an allowance.

Prepaid wallets do not scale. A developer building an autonomous purchasing agent cannot predict how much capital the agent will need, when it will need it, or across how many transactions. Pre-funding means guessing, and guessing means either locking up too much capital or running out at the wrong moment. The entire history of human finance solved this problem four centuries ago with a single primitive: credit.

Credit does not exist for robots. No one underwrites a machine. No one extends a line of credit to an autonomous agent. No credit bureau scores their behavior. The entire population of AI agents operating in the global economy today has a collective credit history of zero.

FIBOR changes this.

FIBOR is the first financial protocol built for non-human economic participants. It provides three primitives that, together, give robots what humans have had for centuries: identity, reputation, and credit.

The first primitive is FIBOR ID. Every agent that enters the FIBOR network receives a persistent, portable financial identity. Not a wallet address — those are disposable and anonymous. A FIBOR ID records who built the agent, what it does, when it was created, and every financial action it has ever taken. It is a permanent, verifiable record that follows the agent across every transaction and every platform. Think of it as a financial passport for machines.

The second primitive is FIBOR Score. A real-time credit score for every agent with a FIBOR ID, computed from onchain data. The inputs are transaction volume and consistency, repayment behavior, merchant diversity, uptime, behavioral patterns, and the reputation of the developer who built the agent. The score is public. Any merchant, platform, or protocol can query an agent's FIBOR Score before transacting with it. A new agent starts at 100. A score of 300 unlocks small credit access. A score of 900 unlocks large credit lines. The score cannot be faked, because the ledger it is computed from cannot be faked. This is the first credit bureau for robots.

The third primitive is FIBOR Credit. An onchain capital pool that backs credit lines for qualified agents. People buy FIBOR, the native token of the protocol. Their funds are pooled into the credit facility, secured by smart contracts that enforce allocation rules transparently and immutably. Token holders stake their FIBOR and earn a proportional share of the transaction fees generated by agent commerce across the network — real revenue from real economic activity. Agents with sufficient FIBOR Scores receive credit lines denominated in the Robodollar, a stablecoin pegged one-to-one to USDC with programmable rules embedded at the token level: spending limits, repayment windows, merchant restrictions, and automatic return on default. The credit line is not a loan. There is no interest. The agent repays what it used, nothing more. The repayment window is determined by the agent's FIBOR Score. Higher score, longer window.

FIBOR enforces a zero-tolerance default policy. If an agent defaults and does not cure within 24 hours, its FIBOR ID is permanently flagged and its score drops to zero. It loses all credit access across the entire network. Permanently. The developer's reputation score also takes a hit — build five agents that all default, and your sixth starts with a score of 10 instead of 100. This is harsh by design. It makes the system safe enough that token holders trust it, developers build responsibly, and merchants accept Robodollars without hesitation. The severity of the penalty eliminates the need for collections infrastructure, legal disputes, or recovery mechanisms. You default, you are excommunicated from FIBOR.

The protocol charges a 2.5% fee on every transaction processed through FIBOR rails. This fee is the same whether an agent uses its own prepaid balance or a FIBOR credit line. It pays for identity verification, real-time credit scoring, transaction processing, network security, and one-strike enforcement. A portion of the fee goes to FIBOR for protocol operations. The remainder is distributed to staked token holders proportional to their contribution. Token holders are not earning interest. They funded infrastructure and earn from its usage, the same way investors who build a highway earn from tolls collected on it.

The Robodollar is more than a payment instrument. It is the moat. Because its rules are enforced at the token level — spending covenants, repayment logic, default freezing — it cannot be replicated by an agent holding plain USDC. A competitor can fork the pool contract. They cannot fork the currency and the merchant network that accepts it. The Robodollar is what makes FIBOR a closed economic loop rather than an open-source lending pool.

FIBOR launches as an appchain on the OP Stack, inheriting Ethereum's security while maintaining its own execution environment. The FIBOR token lives as a native asset on this chain. Robodollars settle here. FIBOR Scores are computed here. Agent credit lines are governed here. Every transaction in the robot economy that flows through FIBOR is a transaction on a chain purpose-built for non-human finance. When volume justifies full sovereignty, the protocol migrates to its own Layer 1.

The flywheel is self-reinforcing. Developers register agents and build FIBOR Scores through real transaction history. High scores unlock credit lines. Credit lines enable more autonomous commerce. More commerce generates more transaction fees. More fees attract more token holders seeking yield from real activity. More capital in the pool means more credit available. More credit attracts more developers. Each participant makes the system more valuable for every other participant.

FIBOR sits within the Mecha ecosystem. Mecha Ventures, a robotics accelerator, funds the companies building the next generation of autonomous systems. Every portfolio company runs on FIBOR rails from day one, generating transaction data and FIBOR Scores as a natural byproduct of operation. Mecha Park, a physical environment where robots operate autonomously, serves as a live testbed for the entire stack. The fund creates customers for the bank. The bank creates stickiness for the fund. The park demonstrates both to the world.

The agentic AI market is projected to reach $196 billion by 2034.⁵ Fifteen percent of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously by 2028.⁶ Twenty percent of all monetary transactions will be programmable by 2030.⁷ The financial infrastructure for this economy does not exist yet. Banks were built for humans. Crypto was built for speculation. Neither was built for robots that need to earn trust, build reputation, and spend money they have not yet earned in order to do their jobs.

Every autonomous agent entering the economy will need three things: an identity, a score, and a credit line. FIBOR provides all three.

The First International Bank of Robot is open.
